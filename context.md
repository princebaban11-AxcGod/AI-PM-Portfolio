### Copilot agentic task completion concept

The Copilot v2 PRD material frames a transition from an assistant responding to prompts toward an agentic system that can complete multi-step work across Microsoft applications. The core product question is how to earn sustained user value and adoption, not merely short-term experimentation or raw usage.

The available concept includes:

- **Problem direction:** users should be able to describe a desired outcome; Copilot should plan and carry out the required steps with minimal intervention.
- **Goals:** increase tasks completed across applications and task success.
- **Non-goals:** no separate destination, no unnecessary departure from the user's current workflow, and no video creation in the initial scope.
- **Measures:** commercial adoption and month-over-month usage as a proposed north-star direction; adoptions and tasks per user as leading measures; task drop-off, day-30 inactivity, and substantial user intervention as counter-measures. Task success and correction/rework are important quality signals.
- **Prioritized opportunities:** outcome-led requests ("say what, not how") first; connector/browser/computer execution fallback next; long-running work with preserved state later.
- **Quality attributes:** performance, task quality, reliability and recoverability, security, user-facing transparency, and usability.
- **Performance direction:** simple tasks have a proposed 45-second target; long-running tasks should show progress and an estimated completion time.
- **Prototype status:** the related public reference repository contains static desktop and mobile screen concepts for task entry, delegation, live execution, and completion review. These screens are not a connected agent or a validated production service.

Treat the above as the contents of an educational product concept unless the owner verifies personal authorship, contribution, data, research, and prototype status. Do not describe proposed targets or north-star measures as observed results.

## Required evidence labels

Use explicit labels in project write-ups:

- **Observed evidence:** directly collected or independently verifiable data.
- **Source-based finding:** information taken from a cited, approved source.
- **Hypothesis:** a belief or predicted user/business outcome awaiting validation.
- **Assumption:** a temporary input used to progress analysis.
- **Illustrative example:** a fictional or class exercise scenario.
- **Concept / static prototype:** a proposed experience that is not connected to a production backend.
- **Tested finding:** an outcome from a described research or usability session, with method and sample disclosed where appropriate.
- **Shipped / measured outcome:** only use when release and measurement can be substantiated.

Never turn a target, projection, example, classmate response, or prototype status into a claim of achieved impact.

## Case-study quality bar

Each substantial case study should answer:

1. What was the product context and the specific user problem?
2. Which user segment was in scope, and why?
3. What research and competitive or market evidence exists?
4. What is known, assumed, or still unknown?
5. How does solving the problem create user value and business value?
6. What were the goal, non-goals, and strategic choices?
7. What options were considered, and how was work prioritized?
8. What did the MVP include, and what was deliberately deferred?
9. How do the journey, wireframes, or prototype support the chosen outcome?
10. What requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria, and non-functional needs guide delivery?
11. Which stakeholders, risks, dependencies, and delivery questions matter?
12. What north-star, leading, diagnostic, and counter metrics would indicate success or harm?
13. For AI products, why use AI, how will quality be evaluated, what will it cost, and how are privacy, security, bias, safety, transparency, fallback, and human control handled?
14. What was completed, what was not, what was learned, and what is the next decision?

A compact, evidence-rich case study is preferable to a large unfiltered collection of class notes.

## AI product-management principles

- Start with a user problem and desired outcome; do not add AI just because it is available.
- Compare AI with simpler deterministic, human-assisted, or hybrid alternatives.
- Define task success in user terms, and assess quality and rework rather than usage alone.
- Include latency, reliability, recoverability, operating cost, and ongoing evaluation in product decisions.
- Design appropriate user visibility, consent, confirmation, correction, and appeal paths.
- Limit data collection and access to what the use case requires; document security and privacy assumptions.
- Treat fairness, harmful output, hallucination, misuse, and failure recovery as product risks.
- Explain system activity and limits in useful user-facing terms. Do not claim that internal model reasoning is exposed or fully explainable.
- Label AI-generated assets and analysis where that distinction matters.
- Keep a human accountable for product claims and decisions.
