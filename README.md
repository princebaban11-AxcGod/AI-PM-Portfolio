## Portfolio focus

The portfolio brings together two connected capabilities:

1. **End-to-end product management:** user and market discovery, problem definition, prioritization, MVP scope, product requirements, roadmaps, go-to-market thinking, metrics, and experimentation.
2. **AI product management:** identifying where AI creates meaningful user value, choosing an appropriate AI experience, accounting for quality and operating cost, and designing for safety, transparency, privacy, user control, and recovery.

The goal is not to publish every class exercise. It is to curate a small number of clear case studies that explain the problem, the decisions made, the evidence behind them, the work produced, and what remains unvalidated.

Explore a Copilot experience in which people describe the outcome they want and Copilot plans and completes multi-step work in their existing application workflow.

**Portfolio treatment:** explain your own contribution and decision-making; distinguish the written concept and static screens from a working agent or validated product. Link to external course/reference material only as a reference, not as your work unless authorship is confirmed.

### 2. Product discovery and user research

Curate a focused example showing the path from a broad problem to research questions, interviews or survey design, synthesis, an evidence-based persona, and a user journey. Possible learning examples in the materials include emergency routing and an experience that helps children understand saving and investing with virtual coins.

### 3. Product requirements and roadmap

The course materials include PRD and roadmap exercises, including a RedBus concept addressing what happens when a traveler misses a booked bus and needs a timely alternative.

**Portfolio treatment:** show the problem statement, goals and non-goals, user needs, requirements, acceptance criteria, prioritization, dependencies, and roadmap choices. Identify invented assumptions and validation gaps.

### 4. Go-to-market, lifecycle, and growth

Possible examples include product positioning, a Zomato go-to-market exercise, pricing and business-model analysis, product lifecycle and growth loops, and A/B testing.

**Portfolio treatment:** connect the intended segment and value proposition to channels, adoption, activation, retention, revenue, and guardrail metrics. Do not claim campaign or business results unless measured evidence is available.

## Recommended case-study format

Use this structure for each project:

1. **Project snapshot:** name, dates, role, collaborators, status, and links.
2. **Problem and context:** who experiences the problem, what they are trying to do, and why it matters to users and the business.
3. **Evidence:** research method, sources, sample, findings, limitations, and open questions.
4. **Opportunity and strategy:** target segment, value proposition, market/competitor context, product principles, and alternatives considered.
5. **Scope and trade-offs:** goals, non-goals, MVP, prioritization logic, dependencies, and risks.
6. **Experience:** persona or job-to-be-done, journey, wireframes/prototype, and usability findings.
7. **Requirements and execution:** PRD, user stories, acceptance criteria, stakeholders, delivery plan, and launch approach.
8. **Measurement:** north-star outcome, leading and diagnostic metrics, guardrails, baseline, and measurement plan.
9. **AI-specific considerations (when relevant):** why AI is appropriate, model/tool assumptions, quality evaluation, latency, cost, privacy, safety, transparency, human approval, and fallback behavior.
10. **Outcome and reflection:** what was shipped or tested, verified results, what did not work, and the next decision.
